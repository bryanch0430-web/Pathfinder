Kyoto works best when you accept that the famous sites are spread out. Higashiyama is a walking district with a real slope: start early at Kiyomizu-dera and walk downhill toward Gion rather than reversing the direction in the afternoon heat. Arashiyama is a separate half-day; do not put it between two Higashiyama temples.

Nishiki Market is a grazing stop, not a full meal. Most stalls close by late afternoon, so pair it with a proper lunch nearby and keep dinner for a neighbourhood restaurant. The same rule applies to tea houses: one deliberate stop is better than five rushed ones.

Buses are useful but slow in peak season. The subway and a short taxi can save a lot of standing time when a route crosses the city. For temples, arrive at opening or in the last hour; the middle of the day is for gardens, cafés and indoor museums.

Dress for removal: many temples and some restaurants expect shoes off. Carry a light layer, a small towel and cash for small shops. Kyoto rewards fewer stops and longer pauses.
