Tokyo looks compact on a map, but station transfers are the real cost. Group each day by rail corridor instead of by “top ten” list. Asakusa, Ueno and Yanaka form a coherent east-side day; Shibuya, Harajuku and Shinjuku belong together on the west. Mixing the two in one afternoon means losing most of it underground.

Use an IC card and treat the first and last train times as hard constraints. Rush hour is genuinely uncomfortable with luggage; use a airport limousine bus or a later train if you arrive in the morning peak. Large stations have multiple exits, so navigate to the exit name, not just the station name.

Tokyo is a walking city between stations, but not a continuous walking city. Build in one neighbourhood pause rather than six photographed crossings. Museums and popular observation decks benefit from a reserved time slot; leave the rest of the day flexible.

For a first visit, two bases are enough: one east-side morning and one west-side afternoon, with a slower day for a garden, a shrine and a long lunch. Save day trips for a separate day rather than bolting them onto a city day.
