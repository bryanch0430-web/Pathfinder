New York is best planned by neighbourhood, not by attraction count. Lower Manhattan, SoHo and the West Village connect naturally on foot; the Upper East Side and Central Park form another day. Trying to walk from the Financial District to Harlem and back is a recipe for a tired, shallow visit.

The subway is the default tool, but station entrances and uptown/downtown platforms matter. Check the direction before tapping in. For short crosstown trips, walking is often faster than descending into a station, especially in Midtown.

Museums deserve a time block, not a “quick stop”. The Met and the American Museum of Natural History can each absorb half a day. Book timed entries where offered; leave the rest of the day light.

New York is expensive and tipping is expected in sit-down restaurants and bars. Prices on menus often exclude tax and tip. Carry a card, but keep a little cash for small vendors. In summer, heat and subway platforms are more draining than the distance suggests; plan an indoor pause in the afternoon.
