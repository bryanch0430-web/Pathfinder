San Francisco is compact but hilly, and the hills change the walking plan. A route that looks short on a map can include two steep climbs. Use a cable car or bus for the uphill leg and walk down; do not schedule a full day of hills without a break.

Fisherman's Wharf, the Ferry Building and the Embarcadero are easy to chain, but they are tourist-heavy. The Mission, Hayes Valley and the Inner Richmond give a better sense of how the city lives. Alcatraz needs a timed ferry and should be booked ahead; treat it as a half-day, not an add-on.

The fog is a real scheduling factor. The western neighbourhoods and the bridge can be grey while the Mission is sunny. Keep one outdoor viewpoint flexible and swap it if the bridge disappears. Layering matters more than a heavy coat.

Transit is useful for cross-town trips, but the historic cable cars are slow and crowded. Use them as an experience, not as your primary transport. Tipping follows US norms; taxis and sit-down meals expect it.
