Lantau is the counterweight to Kowloon's density: big landscapes, a cable car, a hilltop Buddha and a fishing village. Give it a full day rather than squeezing it between two urban stops.

Start early. Ngong Ping Village and the Tian Tan Buddha are calmer before mid-morning, and the cable-car queue grows quickly after 10:00. The 25-minute ride is part of the experience; if the cable car is closed for weather, the bus up is the practical fallback, not a reason to abandon the day.

After the Big Buddha, move on to Tai O instead of returning immediately to the city. The stilt-house village is at its best in the afternoon, and the bus between Ngong Ping and Tai O is a slow but scenic link. Finish with an early dinner in Tai O or back in Tung Chung.

Practical rhythm: cable car up, Buddha and village on foot, bus to Tai O, bus back to Tung Chung. Keep an hour of slack for queues and weather. Lantau is cooler and windier than the harbourfront; bring a layer outside summer.
