Japan's rail network is excellent, but the pass is not automatically the cheapest option. Compare a JR Pass against point-to-point tickets for the exact route and dates; a pass only pays off on enough long-distance travel. Reserve popular shinkansen seats early, especially in holiday periods.

IC cards work across most urban trains, subways and buses, and save time at ticket machines. Keep one card rather than buying a paper ticket for every ride. Airport transfers are the exception: a dedicated express train or limousine bus can be simpler with luggage than a chain of local trains.

Luggage forwarding is normal and useful. Send large bags ahead to the next hotel and travel with a small overnight bag for one or two nights. Coin lockers at major stations fill early; do not assume one will be free at midday.

Etiquette is mostly ordinary consideration: queue, keep phone calls off trains, do not eat while walking in crowded areas, and follow the marked side of an escalator. Tipping is not expected. Carry cash for small businesses and rural areas, even if cards are widely accepted in cities.
